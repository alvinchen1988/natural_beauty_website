Perimenopause and Menopause Care

Care That Starts with Your Symptoms and Health History

Perimenopause is the transition leading to menopause and can begin years before the final menstrual period. Menopause is identified after 12 consecutive months without menstruation when no other cause is present. Symptoms and timing vary widely.

Women’s Health & Wellness

Common Reasons to Seek Care

  • Hot flashes and night sweats
  • Sleep disruption
  • Menstrual changes during perimenopause
  • Mood changes, irritability or anxiety
  • Brain fog or concentration concerns
  • Vaginal dryness or discomfort
  • Urinary or sexual-health changes
  • Changes in body composition
  • Questions about bone, cardiovascular and metabolic health

These symptoms can overlap with thyroid disease, anemia, sleep disorders, medication effects, depression and other medical conditions. Assessment helps determine whether further investigation or referral is needed.

Your Assessment

The consultation may include menstrual and symptom history, medical and family history, medication review, pregnancy considerations when relevant, cardiovascular and breast-health risk review, and discussion of personal goals. Laboratory testing is ordered only when clinically indicated; hormone levels are not always required to identify the menopausal transition.

Treatment Options

Care may include education, sleep and lifestyle strategies, non-hormonal medication, local vaginal therapy, systemic hormone therapy for appropriate patients, and referral when needed. The plan is individualized and reviewed over time.

Menopause Care FAQ

Do I need blood tests to confirm perimenopause?+

Not always. Symptoms, age, menstrual history and clinical context are often more useful because hormone levels can fluctuate.

Are all symptoms caused by menopause?+

No. Other medical or psychological conditions can cause similar symptoms and may require investigation.

Can I receive care if I do not want hormones?+

Yes. Non-hormonal and lifestyle options can be discussed according to the symptoms and health history.

Hormone Replacement Therapy

01

Individualized Menopausal Hormone Therapy

Menopausal hormone therapy may help selected patients manage bothersome menopause symptoms. Treatment can include estrogen, with a progestogen when required for endometrial protection, or local vaginal estrogen for selected genitourinary symptoms.

Hormone therapy is not suitable for everyone and is not prescribed solely on request without assessment. Decisions consider symptoms, age, time since menopause, uterus status, medical and family history, cardiovascular and thrombotic risk, breast-health history, medication use and patient preferences.

02

Potential Benefits

Depending on the formulation and patient, hormone therapy may reduce hot flashes and night sweats, improve sleep disrupted by vasomotor symptoms, and treat selected vaginal and urinary symptoms. Systemic therapy also affects bone health, but treatment decisions are based on the individual balance of benefits and risks.

03

Assessment and Monitoring

Before prescribing, Dina reviews contraindications, relevant screening history and treatment alternatives. The lowest appropriate effective dose and route are considered based on the clinical situation. Follow-up evaluates symptom response, side effects, bleeding patterns, adherence and whether treatment should continue or change.

04

Risks and Precautions

Risks vary by the type, dose, route, timing and duration of therapy and by individual health factors. They may include blood clots, stroke, gallbladder disease, breast-health risks and abnormal bleeding. Any unexplained postmenopausal bleeding requires medical assessment.

Hormone Therapy FAQ

Is bioidentical hormone therapy safer?+

The term can be confusing. Some regulated prescription hormones are chemically identical to human hormones. Custom-compounded products are not automatically safer or more effective and may have different quality-control considerations.

Will hormone therapy cause weight loss?+

Hormone therapy is not a weight-loss treatment. It may improve symptoms that affect sleep or activity, but weight management requires a separate assessment.

How long can I use hormone therapy?+

There is no single duration for everyone. Ongoing use should be reassessed periodically according to symptoms, benefits, risks and preferences.
